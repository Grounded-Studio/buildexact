How the unit converter works

Pick what you convert, type a value and choose its unit. You see the value in every other unit at once, so you never have to look up a factor.

Slope converts between degrees, percent and a ratio of 1:x, and shows the rise per metre. Handy for ramps, roofs and the fall of drains and terraces.

Tip: a fall of 1:100 is 1% or 10 mm per metre, which is the usual minimum for a terrace.

Slope in degrees, percent and 1:x

Drawings, products and regulations all write slope differently. A roof is usually given in degrees, a ramp or drive in percent, and a drain or terrace as a ratio such as 1:100. Percent is the rise per 100 units across: tan(degrees) × 100. The ratio 1:x is 100 ÷ percent.

Some useful values: a drain often needs 1:100 to 1:50 (10 to 20 mm per metre), a wheelchair ramp is often limited to around 1:12 to 1:20, and a roof of 30° rises 577 mm per metre.

Imperial and metric

Products from the UK and the US still come in inches, feet and gallons, while drawings in most of Europe are in millimetres. Working in one system and converting at the end avoids rounding errors: convert the final quantity, not every measurement along the way.

Frequently asked questions

How many square feet is 1 m²?

1 m² is 10.764 square feet. 1 square foot is 0.0929 m².

How do I convert a slope in degrees to percent?

Percent = tan(degrees) × 100. 30° is 57.7%, 45° is 100%.

What does a slope of 1:50 mean?

1 unit up for every 50 units across: 2% or 20 mm per metre.

How many litres are in a cubic foot?

28.3 litres. A cubic metre is 35.3 cubic feet.

What is 1:12 in percent and degrees?

8.3%, or 4.8 degrees.
