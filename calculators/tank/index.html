How the tank volume calculator works

Pick the shape and enter the inside dimensions in cm. Upright round tanks use π × r² × height, rectangular tanks length × width × height.

Enter the liquid level to see how much is in the tank now. For a lying round tank the calculator uses the exact circle segment, because the middle of the tank holds much more per cm than the bottom.

Tip: 1 m³ is 1000 litres. A rain barrel of Ø60 cm and 90 cm high holds about 254 litres.

Worked example: a tank of Ø100 cm

A round tank with an inside diameter of 100 cm and 120 cm long:

Upright, fullπ × 50² × 120 ÷ 1000 = 942 litres
Upright, 45 cm of liquidπ × 50² × 45 ÷ 1000 = 353 litres
Lying, full942 litres
Lying, 25 cm of liquid184 litres (20%)
Rectangular 120 × 80 × 100 cm960 litres

Note the lying tank: at a quarter of its height it holds only a fifth of its capacity, because the bottom of a circle is narrow.

Why a lying tank is tricky

In an upright tank every centimetre holds the same number of litres, so the contents rise in a straight line. In a lying tank the width changes with the height: narrow at the bottom, widest in the middle and narrow again at the top. A dipstick reading therefore cannot simply be multiplied.

The calculator uses the area of the circle segment below the liquid level, times the length of the tank. That gives the exact litres at any level, which is handy for heating oil, diesel and rainwater tanks.

Measure on the inside

Walls, ribs and fittings take up space, so the inside dimensions give the real capacity. If you can only measure the outside, subtract twice the wall thickness from the diameter. Tanks are rarely filled to the very top; most have an air gap of a few centimetres.

Frequently asked questions

How do I calculate the litres in a round tank?

π × radius² × height in dm. A tank of Ø100 cm and 100 cm high: 3.1416 × 5² × 10 = 785 litres.

How much is in a lying tank that is half full?

Exactly half of the capacity. Above or below half the litres per cm change; enter the level and the calculator does the segment maths.

How many litres is 1 m³?

1000 litres. 1 litre is 1 dm³.

How many litres are in a tank of 1 × 1 × 1 m?

1000 litres: 1 m³ is exactly 1000 litres.

How do I measure the liquid level?

Lower a straight stick or measuring rod to the bottom, pull it out and read where it is wet, in cm from the bottom.
