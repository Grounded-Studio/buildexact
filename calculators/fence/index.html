How the fence calculator works

Enter the length of each fence line in metres, the post width and the panel width. For an L- or U-shaped fence, add corners and give each line its own length. The calculator places a post, then a panel, then a post, and so on, until the line is full.

What is left at the end becomes an end-cut panel, shortened from a full one, or you can split it into two equal end-cuts at both ends for a symmetrical look. Gates get their own width and beam thickness, with posts on both sides and a top beam.

Margin tip: order one extra panel when you need an end-cut, so you can cut it to size without risk.

Frequently asked questions

How many fence posts do I need?

Roughly one more post than panels on each straight line, plus extra posts for corners and gates. For 14.5 m with 180 cm panels and 10 cm posts that is 9 posts.

What is an end-cut panel?

When the line length is not an exact number of panels, the last gap is filled with a panel cut to size. The calculator tells you how wide it must be.

Can I put the cut panels at both ends?

Yes. Turn on 'two equal end-cuts' and the calculator splits the leftover length over both ends, so the fence looks symmetrical.
