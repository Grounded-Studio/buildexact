How the insulation calculator works

Add one or more layers, each with its own thickness and lambda (λ, the thermal conductivity on the product). The R-value of a layer is thickness in metres ÷ lambda, and the layers are added together into a total R-value.

For each layer the calculator also works out how many packs you need for the area, including cut waste, and adds fasteners and tape per m². That makes it easy to compare a single thick layer with two thinner ones.

Margin tip: 5–10% extra for cutting around rafters, joists and pipes. Seal every seam with tape; gaps cost more insulation value than a slightly thinner layer.

Frequently asked questions

How do I calculate the R-value of insulation?

Divide the thickness in metres by the lambda value. 100 mm of insulation with λ 0.035 gives 0.10 ÷ 0.035 = R 2.86 m²K/W.

What does the lambda value mean?

Lambda (λ) is how easily heat passes through the material. The lower the number, the better it insulates per centimetre.

What R-value do I need?

That depends on the part of the house and on your local building rules. Roofs and floors usually need a higher R-value than older walls allow; check the requirements where you build.
