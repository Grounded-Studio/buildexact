How the excavation calculator works

Choose a pit, a trench or round holes and enter the size and depth. The calculator gives the solid volume you dig out in m³.

Dug soil takes up more room than it did in the ground: that is bulking, usually 20-30% for sand and up to 40% for clay. The loose volume decides how many skips you need. The weight in tonnes uses the soil weight you enter.

Tip: a 6 m³ skip may not always be filled to the top with heavy soil because of its weight limit. Ask your supplier.

Worked example: a pit of 6 × 4 m

A pit of 6 × 4 m and 0.6 m deep in sandy soil (1.6 t/m³), with 25% bulking and skips of 6 m³:

Solid volume6 × 4 × 0.6 = 14.4 m³
Loose volume14.4 × 1.25 = 18.0 m³
Weight14.4 × 1.6 = 23 tonnes
Skips of 6 m³18.0 ÷ 6 = 3 skips

That is about 7.7 tonnes per skip. Many skips have a weight limit, so check with the hire company before you fill them to the top.

Pit, trench or round holes

A pit is a rectangle: length × width × depth. A trench is the same sum, with the length of all trenches added up and the width of your bucket. Round holes for posts or piles use π × r² × depth per hole, times the number of holes.

Always measure the depth from the current ground level to the bottom you need, including the layer of sand or gravel that goes under the foundation.

Bulking and soil weight

Soil in the ground is packed. Once dug it loosens and takes up 20 to 40% more room: sand bulks least, clay most. Skips and trucks carry the loose volume, so that is what you plan removal with.

The weight depends on the soil and how wet it is. Dry sand is about 1.6 t/m³, wet clay 1.9 t/m³ or more. Trucks are usually limited by weight, not by volume.

Frequently asked questions

How do I calculate excavation volume?

Length × width × depth, all in metres. A pit of 6 × 4 m and 0.6 m deep is 14.4 m³ of solid soil.

What is bulking of soil?

Soil loosens when you dig it. 1 m³ in the ground becomes about 1.2 to 1.4 m³ loose. Sand bulks less, clay more.

How much does 1 m³ of soil weigh?

Roughly 1.5 to 1.9 tonnes. Dry sand is around 1.6 t/m³, wet clay can be 1.9 t/m³ or more.

How many skips do I need for 10 m³ of soil?

With 25% bulking 10 m³ becomes 12.5 m³ loose, so 3 skips of 6 m³ (or 2 skips of 6 m³ plus a small one).

How deep should a foundation trench be?

In many places at least 60 to 80 cm, below the frost line and down to firm ground. Follow the drawings or the engineer's spec.
