How the cost estimator works

Add a line for every part of the job: materials, labour, hire and transport. Each line is quantity × unit price. The estimator adds them up, applies an optional discount and adds VAT at the rate you enter.

Use it for a quick price check or a first estimate for a client. For a quote with your logo, the client's details and a signature, build it in the BuildExact app and share it as a PDF.

Tip: list labour as separate lines, so a client can see what the work costs versus the materials.

Worked example: a small bathroom floor

Four lines, a 5% discount and 21% VAT:

Floor tiles6 m² × €35 = €210.00
Tile adhesive3 bags × €18 = €54.00
Labour16 h × €55 = €880.00
Skip hire1 × €250 = €250.00
Subtotal€1,394.00
5% discount−€69.70 → €1,324.30
21% VAT+€278.10 → €1,602.40

The client sees €1,602.40 including VAT, and the labour line shows what the work itself costs.

What belongs in an estimate

Materials including waste, labour per task, travel or a call-out charge, equipment hire and waste disposal. On older buildings add a contingency of 5 to 10% for what you only find once you open up a wall or floor, and say so on the quote.

Markup or margin?

Markup is added on top of your cost; margin is the share of the selling price you keep. They are not the same number, and mixing them up quietly costs money:

Markup on costMargin on price
10%9.1%
20%16.7%
25%20%
33.3%25%
50%33.3%

Common mistakes

Prices without VAT for private clients: consumers compare totals, so show the total including VAT. VAT rates differ per country and sometimes per type of work, so check the rate that applies to your job.

Old material prices: check current prices before a big quote, especially for timber, insulation and metal.

Frequently asked questions

How do I make a quick construction estimate?

List every material and every task with a quantity and a unit price, add them up, then add VAT. Keep a contingency for surprises.

Which VAT rate should I use?

The rate that applies in your country for this type of work. Some countries have a reduced rate for certain renovation work; check the local rules.

Should I include a contingency?

For renovation work, yes. 10–15% is common, shown as a separate line rather than hidden in the prices.

How do I add VAT to a price?

Multiply by 1 plus the rate. €1,000 at 21% VAT is €1,000 × 1.21 = €1,210.

How much contingency should I add?

5 to 10% on older buildings, where you find problems only once a wall or floor is open. Show it as its own line.

What is the difference between markup and margin?

Markup is on top of your cost, margin is part of the selling price. A 25% markup gives a 20% margin.
