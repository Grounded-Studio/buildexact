How the wood calculator works

The wood calculator has three modes. Volume gives the cubic metres of a piece of timber from its length, width and height. Planks works the other way round: enter a target volume and the plank size, and it tells you how many planks you need.

Beams is for framing: enter the number and size of the beams and you get the total volume, the length to buy and the screws. Every mode also gives the surface area, with the paint or stain you need at your coverage and number of coats.

Margin tip: add 10% for knots, splits and offcuts, more with rough-sawn timber.

Frequently asked questions

How do I calculate the volume of wood?

Multiply length × width × height in metres. A plank of 2.4 m × 0.14 m × 0.018 m is about 0.006 m³.

How many planks are in one cubic metre?

Divide 1 m³ by the volume of one plank. With the plank above that is about 165 planks.

How much stain do I need for timber?

Work out the surface area of all sides, then divide by the coverage on the tin and multiply by the number of coats. The calculator does this for you.
