How the wood calculator works

The wood calculator has three modes. Volume gives the cubic metres of a piece of timber from its length, width and height. Planks works the other way round: enter a target volume and the plank size, and it tells you how many planks you need.

Beams is for framing: enter the number and size of the beams and you get the total volume, the length to buy and the screws. Every mode also gives the surface area, with the paint or stain you need at your coverage and number of coats.

Margin tip: add 10% for knots, splits and offcuts, more with rough-sawn timber.

Worked example: floor joists

Ten joists of 45 × 145 mm and 4.2 m long, with 10% extra for cuts and rejects:

One joist0.045 × 0.145 × 4.2 = 0.0274 m³
Ten joists0.274 m³
Total length10 × 4.2 = 42 m
With 10% extra42 × 1.10 = 46.2 m

Timber yards price per metre or per m³, so you can compare offers with either number.

Common timber sizes

Sizes differ a little per country and supplier. Lengths usually run from 2.4 to 5.4 m in steps of 30 cm.

Sawn or planed

Planed timber is a few millimetres smaller than its name: a 50 × 150 sawn piece is about 45 × 145 once planed. Enter the real size in the calculator, not the nominal one.

Common mistakes

Wet timber indoors: use kiln-dried timber inside; wet timber shrinks, twists and cracks plaster as it dries.

Untreated timber outside: anything in or near the ground needs treated or naturally durable wood.

No extra for rejects: some pieces will have splits or big knots in the wrong place. 10% covers that for most jobs.

Frequently asked questions

How do I calculate the volume of wood?

Multiply length × width × height in metres. A plank of 2.4 m × 0.14 m × 0.018 m is about 0.006 m³.

How many planks are in one cubic metre?

Divide 1 m³ by the volume of one plank. With the plank above that is about 165 planks.

How much stain do I need for timber?

Work out the surface area of all sides, then divide by the coverage on the tin and multiply by the number of coats. The calculator does this for you.

How do I calculate cubic metres of timber?

Length × width × thickness, all in metres. A beam of 4.2 m × 45 × 145 mm is 4.2 × 0.045 × 0.145 = 0.0274 m³.

How many planks are in a cubic metre?

1 ÷ the volume of one plank. Planks of 18 × 140 mm and 2.4 m long are 0.00605 m³ each, so about 165 per m³.
