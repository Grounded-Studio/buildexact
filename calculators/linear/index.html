When to use linear metres

Linear metres (running metres) are used for anything sold by length: skirting boards, cornices, edging, cable trunking, sealant joints and trim. Measure every run and add them together.

Products often come in fixed lengths, for example 2.4 m skirting. Divide the total by the length of one piece and round up, and keep an eye on joints: put them where they are least visible.

Margin tip: add 5–10% for mitres in corners and offcuts.

Worked example: edging along a garden path

Three runs of 6.5 m, 4.2 m and 3.8 m, 5% extra for cuts, edging sold in lengths of 1 m:

Total6.5 + 4.2 + 3.8 = 14.5 m
With 5% extra14.5 × 1.05 = 15.2 m
Lengths of 1 m15.2 → 16 pieces

Round up to whole pieces: you can't buy 0.2 of an edging stone.

How far a cartridge of sealant goes

Sealant is sold by volume, joints are measured in metres. A 310 ml cartridge holds 310,000 mm³; divide by the joint's cross-section to get the metres:

Joint (width × depth)Metres per 310 ml cartridge
5 × 5 mm12.4 m
6 × 6 mm8.6 m
8 × 8 mm4.8 m
10 × 10 mm3.1 m

Architraves and door trim

Each side of a standard door needs two legs of about 2.1 m and a head of about 1 m: around 5.2 m per side, or 10.4 m per doorway if you trim both sides. Buy legs in one piece; a joint halfway up a door frame always shows.

Where to put the joints

Products come in fixed lengths, so long runs need joints. Put them over a stud or batten, away from the eye line, and avoid short pieces under about 50 cm: they look patched and are hard to fix. That is the main reason to keep 5 to 10% extra.

Frequently asked questions

What is a linear metre?

A length measurement in a straight line, regardless of width. Ten metres of skirting is ten linear metres.

How do I measure skirting boards?

Measure the length of every wall at floor level, subtract door openings and add them up.

How many lengths of 2.4 m do I need?

Divide the total linear metres by 2.4 and round up. 17 m ÷ 2.4 = 7.1, so 8 lengths.

What is the difference between linear metres and square metres?

Linear metres measure only length, for things like skirting and trim. Square metres measure area: length × width.

How do I convert metres to feet?

Multiply by 3.281. 10 metres is 32.8 feet; one foot is 0.3048 m.

How much sealant do I need?

Divide 310,000 by the joint size in mm². A 310 ml cartridge fills about 8.6 m of a 6 × 6 mm joint.
