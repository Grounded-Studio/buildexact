How the paint calculator works

Enter the area you want to paint, the number of coats and the coverage from the tin (m² per litre). Litres needed = area × coats ÷ coverage, plus a small waste percentage for rollers, trays and touch-ups.

You can add a primer coat with its own coverage. The result is split into common can sizes, so you know which tins to buy instead of just a number of litres. Spraying uses more paint than a brush or roller, so pick the method you will use.

Margin tip: 5–10% extra is enough for walls. On rough or porous surfaces, such as fresh plaster or brick, coverage can be 30% lower than the tin says.

Frequently asked questions

How much paint do I need for one room?

An average bedroom has about 30–40 m² of wall. With two coats and 10 m² per litre that is 6 to 8 litres, plus a little for waste.

Do I need two coats of paint?

Almost always. One coat rarely covers evenly, especially over a different colour. Fresh plaster also needs a primer or mist coat first.

What does coverage on a paint tin mean?

It is how many square metres one litre covers in a single coat on a smooth surface, often 8 to 12 m²/L. Use the number from your own tin for the best result.
