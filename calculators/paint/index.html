How the paint calculator works

Enter the area you want to paint, the number of coats and the coverage from the tin (m² per litre). Litres needed = area × coats ÷ coverage, plus a small waste percentage for rollers, trays and touch-ups.

You can add a primer coat with its own coverage. The result is split into common can sizes, so you know which tins to buy instead of just a number of litres. Spraying uses more paint than a brush or roller, so pick the method you will use.

Margin tip: 5–10% extra is enough for walls. On rough or porous surfaces, such as fresh plaster or brick, coverage can be 30% lower than the tin says.

Worked example: a bedroom

Walls of a 4 × 3.5 m room, 2.5 m high, minus a door (1.7 m²) and a window (1.4 m²), two coats at 10 m² per litre, 5% waste:

Wall area15 × 2.5 − 1.7 − 1.4 = 34.4 m²
Paint34.4 ÷ 10 × 2 × 1.05 = 7.2 L
To buyone 5 L and one 2.5 L tin (7.5 L)

The ceiling is a separate job: add it as its own area, often with a different paint.

Typical coverage per litre

The tin gives the coverage on a smooth, primed surface. On real walls it is often lower:

Paintm² per litre
Wall paint on smooth walls8–12
Wall paint on rough or porous walls5–8
Gloss or satin on wood12–16
Masonry paint on render4–8

How many coats?

Similar colour: two coats. Dark to light, or a strong colour: a primer or a tinted base plus two coats. Fresh plaster: first a thinned mist coat, because the plaster soaks up the first layer.

Common mistakes

Buying exactly enough: a little left over is useful for touch-ups later. Close the tin well and store it frost-free.

Tins from different batches: pour them together in a bucket before you start, so one wall doesn't come out a shade different.

Working in feet and inches

In Imperial the calculator works in square feet and US gallons. 430 sq ft, two coats at 400 sq ft per gallon and 5% waste need 2.3 gallons: two 1-gallon cans and a quart.

Frequently asked questions

How much paint do I need for one room?

An average bedroom has about 30–40 m² of wall. With two coats and 10 m² per litre that is 6 to 8 litres, plus a little for waste.

Do I need two coats of paint?

Almost always. One coat rarely covers evenly, especially over a different colour. Fresh plaster also needs a primer or mist coat first.

What does coverage on a paint tin mean?

It is how many square metres one litre covers in a single coat on a smooth surface, often 8 to 12 m²/L. Use the number from your own tin for the best result.

How many litres of paint do I need for a room?

For the walls of a 4 × 3.5 m room, 2.5 m high: about 34 m², so two coats at 10 m² per litre is roughly 7 litres.

How many coats of paint do I need?

Usually two. Fresh plaster needs a thinned mist coat first, and a big colour change may need a primer.
