How Accurate Is a Spirit Level App? When Your Phone Is Good Enough

📐 Tools · 📅 November 5, 2026 ⏱ 6 min read ✍️ Grounded Studio

Every tradesperson has a proper spirit level in the van. But for a quick check of a shelf, a picture frame, a window sill or the fall of a terrace, the phone in your pocket is often closer. The question is: how far can you trust it?

How a phone level works

A phone has an accelerometer that measures in which direction gravity pulls. From that the app calculates the angle. Lay the phone flat and you get a bubble level for two directions at once; stand it on its edge and it works like a traditional spirit level along the long side.

How accurate is it?

After calibration, a typical phone is reliable to about 0.1 to 0.2 degrees. Without calibration it can be off by half a degree or more, because the sensor is never mounted perfectly straight in the phone and the case or camera bump tilts it.

AngleSlopeHeight difference per metre
0.1°0.17 %1.7 mm
0.5°0.87 %8.7 mm
1°1.75 %17.5 mm
2°3.5 %35 mm

So a well-calibrated phone gets you to within about 2 mm per metre. Fine for a shelf, a picture or checking whether a terrace has fall. For setting out a long kitchen worktop or a door frame, take the 2-metre level: it averages out bumps in the surface over its full length, and the phone only measures where it lies.

Calibrate in 10 seconds

  1. Lay the phone on a surface you know is level, or as level as you can find.
  2. Tap Calibrate. From now on that position reads as 0.
  3. Check: turn the phone 180° on the same spot. If the reading is the same, the calibration is good. If it shows the opposite value, the surface was not level; calibrate again on a better one.

Take the case off, or calibrate with the case on and always measure with it on. A camera bump on the back makes a difference when the phone lies flat.

💡 Fall in mm per metre

Paving, terraces and shower floors need fall to drain water. A common guideline is 1 to 2 % (10 to 20 mm per metre), but always check the product instructions and local rules. A reading in mm/m saves you converting degrees in your head.

Summary

A calibrated phone level is accurate to about 2 mm per metre: good enough for quick checks, shelves, frames and fall, not for setting out long, precise work. Calibrate on a level surface, verify by turning 180° and read the fall directly in mm per metre.
