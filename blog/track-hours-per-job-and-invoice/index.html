Track Hours per Job and Invoice Them Without Losing a Quarter Hour

⏱️ Hours · 📅 November 5, 2026 ⏱ 6 min read ✍️ Grounded Studio

Ask a tradesperson on Friday how many hours they spent on Tuesday's job and you get a guess. And guesses are almost always too low: the drive to the supplier, the half hour of waiting, the extra trip for one missing fitting. Over a year that is easily a few weeks of work you did but never invoiced.

Why hours from memory cost you money

Hours you write down afterwards are rounded in the client's favour without you noticing. Fifteen minutes here, half an hour there. At €50 an hour, fifteen forgotten minutes a day is about €2,500 a year for a full-time tradesperson. The fix is not working harder, it is recording time while it happens.

Three ways to track hours

MethodProsCons
Notebook or notes appSimple, always thereDone afterwards, easy to forget, has to be typed over
SpreadsheetTotals per clientOnly at the end of the day or week
Timer per jobExact, no extra workYou have to press start and stop

Rounding: fair for both sides

Most tradespeople bill per quarter hour. Round to the nearest quarter, not always up: 3 hours and 5 minutes becomes 3.00, 3 hours and 10 minutes becomes 3.25. Clients accept that much more easily than a bill where every number is suspiciously round, and you still never give away a full quarter.

💡 Start the timer when you leave, not when you arrive

If travel time and buying materials for the job are part of your agreement, start the timer when you set off. Agree beforehand with the client whether travel time is billed; then it is never a discussion.

How it works in BuildExact

  1. Open the project and tap Work order & hours.
  2. Tap Start timer when you begin and stop it when you are done. Only one timer runs at a time: starting a timer on another project stops the first one. A run of under a minute counts as a mis-tap and is not saved.
  3. Forgot to start it? Add hours by hand with the + button and pick the day.
  4. Make the invoice. The open hours are added automatically as a labour line at your default hourly rate, rounded to the nearest quarter hour. Once the invoice is saved, those hours are marked as billed, so they never end up on two invoices.

The same hours appear on the work order the client signs, so what they signed is exactly what they are billed.

Summary

Tracking hours with a timer per job costs you two taps a day and stops the slow leak of unbilled time. Round to the nearest quarter, agree on travel time up front and let the open hours flow straight onto the invoice.
