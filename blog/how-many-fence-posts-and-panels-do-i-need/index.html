How Many Fence Posts and Panels Do I Need? Count Your Fence Exactly

🏗️ Calculations · 📅 November 14, 2026 ⏱ 6 min read ✍️ Grounded Studio

Dividing the length of your fence by the panel width gives you the wrong answer. The posts take up room too, the last panel almost never fits and every corner and gate changes the count. Here is how to get it right before you order.

Why length ÷ panel width is wrong

A 12 m fence with 180 cm panels looks like 12 ÷ 1.8 = 6.7 panels. But between every two panels there is a post, and with 10 cm posts those add up to almost a metre over that length. Forget them and you end up with a gap at the end, or with one panel too many.

The formula for a straight fence

For a straight run with a post at both ends, each panel needs its own width plus one post:

  • Panels = (length − post width) ÷ (panel width + post width), rounded up
  • Posts = panels + 1

The last panel is usually shorter: you cut a full panel to size. That cut piece is called the end panel.

Worked example: 12 m fence

InputValue
Fence length12.00 m
Panel width180 cm
Post width10 cm
  1. (1200 − 10) ÷ (180 + 10) = 6.26, so 7 panels: 6 full ones and 1 end panel.
  2. Posts: 7 + 1 = 8 posts.
  3. End panel: 1200 − 6 × 180 − 8 × 10 = 40 cm.

Check: 6 × 180 + 40 + 8 × 10 = 1200 cm. It fits exactly.

💡 Two equal end panels look better

A 40 cm piece at one end catches the eye. You can also split the leftover over both ends. For the same 12 m that gives 5 full panels and two end panels of 110 cm, still with 8 posts. Many people find that tidier, especially along a back line that everyone sees.

Corners and gates

  • Corners: measure every straight line separately. A corner post belongs to both lines, so count it once. An L-shape is 2 lines with 1 corner, a U-shape 3 lines with 2 corners.
  • Gates: a gate takes up its own width plus the beams or frame on both sides. It needs a post on each side. These are often heavier, or posts without a groove for panels.
  • Very small gaps: an end panel of just a few centimetres is not worth cutting. Move a post or use slightly wider panels instead.

How long should the posts be?

Put at least 60 cm of the post in the ground, and more in soft soil or in a spot that catches a lot of wind. For a 180 cm high fence that means posts of 240 to 270 cm. In concrete, dig a hole about 25 × 25 cm. Work out the volume of the hole, subtract the post, and check on the bag how many litres a bag of postmix or concrete makes.

Summary

Count each panel together with one post, round up, add one post for the end and cut the last panel to size. Measure each line separately, count corner posts once and give gates their own posts. That way you order exactly what you need.
